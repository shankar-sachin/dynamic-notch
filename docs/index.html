What it does

Six things, in the space you already had.

Every one lives in the same black shape, so nothing new competes for room on your screen.

♫

Now Playing

Album art in one ear, a visualiser in the other. Open it for a scrubber and transport. Music and Spotify.

◷

Timer & Stopwatch

A ring that drains beside the time. Timers running in Apple's Clock app are mirrored here too.

⌁

Volume & brightness

An inline bar in the notch, instead of the blurry square in the middle of your screen.

⇩

File shelf

Drag files at the notch and it opens to catch them. Drag them back out into any app.

◎

AirPods

Connect them and it throws itself open: the case turning on the spot, buds lifting out, left, right and case charge.

⚡︎

Ambient events

Charger in, battery low, a padlock when you lock, a dot while the microphone is live.

How it's built

The window never resizes.

That's the whole trick. The panel is permanently the size of its largest state, transparent, and click-through everywhere it isn't drawing — so your menu bar stays usable. Every open, close and morph is a shape animating inside a window that never moves, which is why it flows like one object instead of stepping a frame at a time.

At rest it is the exact size of your notch, in the same dead black, with zero corner flare. Every spring in the app is defined in one file, so the whole surface accelerates and settles together.

Now playingApple events, plus each app's distributed notifications for instant reactions. No private APIs.
VolumeCoreAudio property listeners — event-driven, no polling.
BrightnessPolled, with an ambient-light filter: the sensor ramps, a key press is one jump out of stillness.
AirPods batterysystem_profiler, which reports left, right and case separately.
HoverGlobal NSEvent monitors — no Accessibility prompt.
PermissionsOne, for Automation, and only so it can ask Music and Spotify what's playing.

Install

Drag it to Applications.

Download the disk image, drag Dynamic Notch across, and open it. There's no Dock icon — look in the menu bar, and look up.

macOS will refuse to open it the first time.

This app isn't notarised — that needs a paid Apple Developer account — so Gatekeeper has nothing to check it against. Every unnotarised app gets the same dialog; it isn't a warning about this one in particular. Go to System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down, click Open Anyway, and open it again. Or, from Terminal: 

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/Dynamic Notch.app"
